## Earlier revisions (historical)

`scripts/assets/floating_fortress.py` builds original geometry inspired by the
feel of a floating fortress, not extracted Tribes geometry or textures. Skybreak
is its first consumer. The asset owns the tower, deck, keel, lift pods, recovery
pads, interior ramps/galleries, flag chamber and equipment mounts.

Revision v4 supersedes the guessed v2/v3 layout with measured proportions:
88 x 104 m deck, tapered lower tower, offset upper tower, deep keel and stacked
routes at -6, 0, 7, 14, 25, 31, 38, 45 and 57 m. The flag is in the front room
at 14 m. The 7 m equipment hall has a surrounding 14 m gallery, while the 25 m
armory and 38 m generator storey have separate access ramps. Crown roof is 67 m.
Slabs meet at edges; do not hide coplanar overlap with renderer depth bias.

Revision v5 (silhouette pass) adds a flared hull skirt around the deck edge,
pylon-mounted exterior turrets at 26 m instead of deck-tip mounts, and a keel
that tapers to a near-point at -68 m instead of a flat cap. Its tower crown
went through two wrong attempts before landing on the current one: first a
narrowing top that was too aggressive, then (going off compressed gameplay
video) a flare that widened toward the top — both wrong. The private
in-engine Broadside reference pack's own collision mesh (see
`broadside-private-reference.md`) gives ground truth: the tower narrows in
tiers — constant 24 x 26 m neck to 45 m (holding the existing 25/31/38/45 m

a 6 x 6 m spire base by 70 m. See `broadside-reference-study.md`'s
in-engine-measurement section for the method and exact numbers.

Local reference found in the CrossOver `winxp-dos` bottle under

## Placing it on another map

Use the original map kit's `Mesh`, set `mesh.origin` and `mesh.yaw` (radians),
then call `floating_fortress.build(mesh, team, circuit)`. Team must be 0 or 1;
each independent base should receive a distinct nonempty power-circuit name.
The caller owns terrain, world placement, map metadata and texture/audio packing.
Dimensions are fixed in metres; arbitrary scaling is intentionally not exposed
because player clearance and ramp angles are tested at this scale.

The returned dictionary contains **local** flag, spawn, two entrance, hall and
loft anchors. Transform these through the same mesh transform before writing
world-space metadata. `scripts/build-skybreak.py` demonstrates this, recording
the prefab ID and source hash in the compiled manifest. Do not copy its geometry
into another map compiler. Equipment IDs are unique within a shared mesh;
ownership and power circuits are assigned per instance.

Each base has two exterior plasma turrets, a roof sensor, two generators on the
38 m storey, inventory stations and a repair station using existing simulation
behavior. There is no separate new ammo-station mechanic in this asset.
Blue lift elements are visual geometry, not physical thrusters.

## Validation

### Optional original docking prototype

`scripts/assets/docking_bay.py` adds a receiving bay 12 m below the deck,
an 8 m wide sheltered ramp, team guide lights and two decorative lift pods.
It connects to the existing entrance deck, not directly to the armory. No
reference geometry/textures are used. Movement and the default asset are unchanged.
Build a fresh pack with `scripts/build-skybreak.py OUTPUT --docking` using the
existing Python environment. Load OUTPUT through `PEAKRUNNER_MAP_PACK` and
select the Raindance override slot, not embedded Skybreak.

Five Python asset tests pass, including three-lane vertical support/headroom
sampling of the new approach. `QA_DOCKING=1` with the private pack override
runs an in-game GPU interior capture; visually inspected. This does not prove
body-sweep traversal, bot navigation or competitive balance. Imported Broadside
baseline and public assets remain untouched.

- `python3 scripts/test-floating-fortress.py`: arbitrary translation/rotation,
  anchor stability, triangle budget, unique equipment IDs and independent power.
- `cargo test --locked -p peakrunner-core fortress_`: floor support and
  headroom along each ascending route and flag corridors, ceiling/door sweeps.
- `cargo test --locked -p peakrunner-core skybreak_flags`: spawn/flag checks.
- `QA_SKYBREAK=1 cargo test --locked -p peakrunner --lib render_grass_captures -- --ignored`:
  GPU exterior, entrance, flag room, loft, armory and generator screenshots.

Render and collision triangles come from the same mesh operations. Regenerate
Skybreak into a fresh directory with `scripts/build-skybreak.py`, then replace
its compiled pack together; never hand-edit only collision or visible vertices.
Changing the embedded pack changes network compatibility. Keep public clients
and servers untouched until a coordinated release is ready.

Automated geometric checks do not prove competitive balance, bot navigation or
every jet approach. Recovery-pad routes and a full human capture round still
need playtesting before merging this feature branch as finished.
